Embedded Systems Engineer

Ocean Data System (ODS)

  • Permanent contract

Since 1991, ODS has designed marine electronics and software born from offshore racing: anti-capsize systems, keel management controllers, onboard data loggers, wireless load measurement, embedded HMIs. Equipment found on racing yachts, maxis and superyachts.

What the open sea demands

  • Humidity, salt, vibration
  • Noisy 12/24 V supply
  • No servicing possible at sea
  • Reliability and degraded modes before performance

One product, from first schematic to field feedback

In a small team, the role covers the whole product life cycle, with no hand-off between design office, production and support. I see the failure at the customer’s, and I am the one who fixes the board or the firmware.

What I do

  • 01
    Embedded firmware

    Development and maintenance of the onboard systems firmware, in C# on .NET Micro Framework and TinyCLR (400 MHz ARM9 target). A tightly constrained managed runtime: concurrency through threads, timers and locks, non-deterministic garbage collection. Hence producer/consumer ring buffers and allocations kept to a minimum in acquisition loops.

  • 02
    Safety systems

    Heel monitoring and anti-capsize detection, alarms, man overboard, watchdog. Every change is assessed from the degraded-mode angle: an optimisation that weakens an alarm or a fault detection is a defect.

  • 03
    Multi-sensor acquisition

    Inertial attitude unit (AHRS), wireless sensors, CAN keypads: integration on CAN/J1939, NMEA 0183 and NMEA 2000 buses, RS-232/422/485 serial links, Modbus and BLE. Drivers, timestamping, logging and monitoring.

  • 04
    Electronics design

    Schematic and layout in KiCad and Eagle: revisions of production boards and a full redesign of a control board (12/24/48 V supply, second independent CAN port). One product owned from board to firmware: embedded HTTP and FTP servers, Wi-Fi access point, configuration dashboard.

  • 05
    Hardware and software debugging

    The most expensive defects hide at the boundary between the two. Oscilloscope and logic analyser, bus integrity, noisy supply, datasheets checked against real conditions (switching time versus bit time) — then the fix, in the firmware or on the board.

  • 06
    Production, commissioning, support

    Assembly, soldering and testing before shipping; onboard integration and validation; remote diagnosis with crews, skippers and shipyards. The same rigour on the phone as in the lab.

04About

From signal to decision, from schematic to the field

An engineer with two degrees — ENSEEIHT in Toulouse (electronics and AI for embedded systems) and EHTP in Casablanca (electrical engineering) — I work where software meets physics: a signal to acquire, memory counted in kilobytes, a real-time deadline to meet.

At Schaeffler and NXP, I carried complete embedded AI chains, from radar acquisition to inference on a microcontroller. At Ocean Data System, the scope widened to the whole product: the board, the firmware, commissioning and support, for equipment that has to hold at sea with no possible intervention.

What drives me: taking a hard problem, understanding what the measurement really says, and building the whole chain until it works in the field. Where I am heading: embedded AI, specifically — designing, optimising and deploying models on constrained targets.

How I work

  1. 01

    Facts before interpretation

    I restate the symptom before looking for a solution, and I only conclude on a demonstrated cause.

  2. 02

    Measure rather than estimate

    Latency, memory, detection rate: every result is measured on the target, not assumed.

  3. 03

    Think degraded mode

    What goes to sea cannot be patched remotely. Every change comes with the list of what must be retested.

  4. 04

    From the lab to the field

    A model at 99% in validation is only a start: I go and measure the gap in real conditions, and I look for its cause.
